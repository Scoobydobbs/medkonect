Prototype for the council review · December 2026

MedConnect build map

A healthcare staffing prototype where the medical or nursing council issues each doctor's and nurse's registration as a tamper-evident digital credential. Hospitals verify it by QR against a shared ledger, and when the council revokes it, every hospital sees the change at once.

How many apps?

Three that people use: the provider phone app, the web portals and the public verifier. Behind them sit the API, the ledger network and two data stores.

Where does the chaincode run?

On every organisation's peer. Each chaincode runs as its own container beside the peers; in the demo all of them are on the laptop.

Where is the admin dashboard?

It is the /ops area of the web portals, run by the platform. In the demo it opens in the laptop's browser.

The December demo

The story we will show

About 12 minutes, with fictional people and hospitals marked DEMO. Each step must pass the automated demo check.

  1. Issue.A council registrar checks an application and issues the credential. The screen shows the ledger transaction.Council portalLedger
  2. Wallet.The doctor receives the credential on their phone.Phone
  3. Post.A DEMO hospital in Pune posts an ICU night shift; a nurse shift is already open.Hospital portal
  4. Match.Ranked candidates, each with a plain-English reason. A revoked doctor and a nurse registered only in another state are excluded, with the reason shown.Hospital portal
  5. Verify.The doctor scans the hospital's one-time QR and unlocks with the phone's own fingerprint lock. Signature and ledger status check out.PhoneVerifierLedger
  6. Consent.Hospital and doctor both give electronic consent to the shift contract; its hash goes on the ledger.Hospital portalPhoneLedger
  7. Attest.After the shift, the hospital issues a completion attestation that appears as work history.Hospital portalPhoneLedger
  8. Revoke.The council revokes another doctor's credential; the public verifier shows REVOKED within seconds.Council portalVerifier
  9. Tamper.A script edits a stored contract; verification shows TAMPERED because the hash no longer matches the ledger.Ledger
  10. Erase.The doctor asks for their account to be deleted; personal data is removed, and the ledger keeps only hashes that can't be tied back to them without a copy of the original documents.PhoneAdmin dashboard
  11. Resilience (optional).Switch the whole marketplace off; the public verifier still answers.Verifier

Privacy by design

What goes on the ledger

On the ledger

  • Random record IDs
  • Hashes of credentials, contracts and attestations
  • Statuses such as ACTIVE or REVOKED
  • Issuer IDs and organisations' public keys
  • Validity dates and transaction times

Off the ledger, encrypted

  • Names, phone numbers, email addresses
  • Registration numbers such as DEMO-MH-000123
  • Contract documents
  • The random salts that link hashes to a person; deleting them breaks the link

No identifying data goes on-chain. The ledger makes records tamper-evident; it does not store who people are.

Process

How we work

Every phase follows the same loop, and the team's meeting notes feed into it.

  1. Team meets

    Discussion notes go into the private notes folder.

  2. Notes are cross-checked

    Claude reads the new notes and flags anything that changes scope or raises a compliance or security question.

  3. Abhishek decides

    Each decision is recorded with its reasons, so the build follows what was agreed.

  4. Plan the phase

    A short plan with steps, tests and risks, approved before any code changes.

  5. Build in small steps

    Claude or the backup models build one step at a time. Lint, type checks, tests and a secret scan run on every step.

  6. Review and hand back

    Work done by the backup models is reviewed by Claude and kept, fixed or reverted.

  7. Close the phase

    Security, ledger and compliance review, progress log, push to the private GitHub repository.

Only synthetic data is used: fictional people and hospitals with a visible DEMO marker. Payments, tokens, insurance and real government or registry connections are outside the prototype.
